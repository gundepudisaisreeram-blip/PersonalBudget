  ```
  Expenses = SUM(eligible OUTFLOW ledger entries) − SUM(eligible INFLOW ledger entries)
  ```
  for the applicable Expense/Refund/Reversal transaction types — Refunds and applicable Reversals reduce the net Expenses figure (worked example: ₹500 Expense outflow + ₹500 Refund inflow = ₹0 net Expenses). This is a single combined NET figure; SEPARATE treatment (the previously-open Option B) is no longer under consideration. **Special case, preserved from Open Decision 14-I's composition rule and restated per the Batch 2 ruling:** a REVERSAL-of-a-TRANSFER remains a two-leg transfer case governed by Open Decision 14-G (below), not by this NET Expense formula — it must not be treated as an ordinary single-leg Expense/Refund/Reversal event merely because OD12 is now frozen. **No longer blocked on any Open Decision.**
- **Transfers vs. Investments — FROZEN, v1.2.1 (Open Decision 5, Option A — mutually exclusive) and FROZEN, v1.2.1 (Open Decision 14-G — selected-scope mathematics).**

Applied consistently in Cash Flow (here) and Trends (§4.4). Mirrors the identical mutually-exclusive partition already certified in Phase 5 for `PaymentObligation` (BR-029/BR-030). **This answers "which TRANSFER transactions belong to Transfers versus Investments" — it does not by itself determine how a transaction's ledger legs behave under the selected account scope; that is Open Decision 14-G, answered separately below.**
  - **Selected-scope mathematics (Open Decision 14-G, all four cases FROZEN):**
    - **Case 1 — both source and destination accounts selected:** NET treatment. The two equal-and-opposite ledger legs net to zero for the combined selected scope (e.g., Account A OUTFLOW ₹500 + Account B INFLOW ₹500, both selected → net Transfer effect = ₹0). This does not delete the transfer or its ledger entries — the transaction remains fully recorded; only its net contribution to the combined-scope Transfer figure is zero.
    - **Case 2 — source selected, destination not selected:** a labeled **"Outbound Transfer"** term. The movement MUST NOT be classified as Expense or Income — selected source + unselected destination = Outbound Transfer, not Expense.
    - **Case 3 — destination selected, source not selected:** a labeled **"Inbound Transfer"** term. MUST NOT be classified as Income or Expense — unselected source + selected destination = Inbound Transfer, not Income.
    - **Case 4 — neither account selected:** the Transfer contributes nothing to the selected report scope (unchanged from prior versions).
    - **Multiple selected accounts:** cases 1–4 apply independently per Transfer, based on whether its own source/destination are members of the resolved selected-account set — no separate fifth mathematical model.
  - **Composition with Open Decision 5, restated:** an Investment-classified Transfer (per OD5) follows the identical Case 1–4 analysis as any ordinary Transfer — classification (Transfers vs. Investments) and selected-scope behavior (NET / Outbound / Inbound / excluded) are independent, composing dimensions, per Open Decision 14-H (unaffected as a composition rule).
  - **No longer blocked on any Open Decision.**

### 4.2 Budget Report

**Category A (line items, verbatim §18):** Budget, Actual, Remaining, Variance, Utilization.

**Formula:** identical to the existing certified `budgets/index.blade.php` presentation (Phase 4) and the Phase 5 Budget Snapshot (unfloored): `Actual = BudgetService::calculateUtilization($budget)`; `Remaining = Money::sub($budget->budget_amount, $actual)` (unfloored, per BR-026); `Variance = Remaining`; `Utilization % = actual / budget_amount * 100`. **No new mathematics — this report is a historical, filterable, multi-period rollup over data `BudgetController`/`BudgetService` already certify, not a new formula.** **Historical period range — FROZEN, v1.3.0 (Open Decision 7 — Overlap semantics):** a `Budget` row belongs to a requested reporting range when `budget.period_start <= report_end AND budget.period_end >= report_start` — i.e., the budget's period *overlaps* the requested range, not merely fully-contains-or-is-contained-by it. ### 4.3 Obligations Report

**Category A (line items, verbatim §18):** Planned, Paid, Partially Paid, Pending, Skipped, Overdue. `PaymentObligation.status` additionally has a `Cancelled` value (certified Phase 4 enum) not named in `02`'s list — included here for completeness since a status-grouped report must account for every possible value, not just the ones `02` happened to enumerate.

**Formula:** a `status`-grouped count/sum over `PaymentObligation` rows in the selected historical period. **Period membership — FROZEN, v1.3.0 (Open Decision 7 — Overlap semantics), identical rule to §4.2:** `obligation.period_start <= report_end AND obligation.period_end >= report_start`. Applied consistently to both Budget and Obligations reports (and any other report explicitly dependent on OD7) — no report-specific alternative.

**Historical status — FROZEN, v1.3.0 (Open Decision 3, Option B — full historical/as-of-period reconstruction via `audit_logs`).** A historical Obligations report determines an obligation's status **as of the relevant historical reporting point**, not simply its current `PaymentObligation.status`. Worked example (unchanged): an obligation due August 5, `PENDING` on that date, paid August 10 (current status `PAID`); a report run for the August 1–7 window must show it as `PENDING`/outstanding for that week, not `PAID`. **Reconstruction rule (frozen):**
1. Baseline state = `PENDING` (established at obligation creation, per the certified creation paths — see §5.1).
2. Consider applicable `OBLIGATION_STATUS_CHANGED` audit records.
3. Consider only transitions at or before the relevant as-of boundary.
4. Select the latest applicable transition.
5. Its resulting status becomes the historical status.
6. If no applicable transition exists, the baseline remains `PENDING`.

**This must not be silently simplified to current status.** Required transition coverage (unchanged from §5.1, preserved): `PENDING → PARTIALLY_PAID`; `PARTIALLY_PAID → PAID`; allocation-removal reversions; `PENDING → SKIPPED`; `PENDING → CANCELLED`; repeated transitions; multiple same-day transitions; timestamp ordering; report-period-boundary correctness. **The Audit Completeness Invariant (§5.1) is explicitly preserved, not weakened by this ruling** — OD3 being frozen to Option B does not mean audit completeness may be assumed without verification; the dedicated audit-completeness testing requirements remain mandatory (§13).

**"Overdue" — FROZEN, v1.3.0 (Open Decision 8, Option C — as of the report period's end date).** An obligation is Overdue for a historical report based on whether it was overdue **as of the selected report period's end date** — the report-period end is the authoritative snapshot boundary. Worked example: obligation due August 5, paid August 10 — for report range `2026-08-01 → 2026-08-31` (end = Aug 31), **not Overdue** (already paid by then); for report range `2026-08-01 → 2026-08-07` (end = Aug 7), **Overdue** (still unpaid at that boundary). **Explicitly not** "was ever overdue during the period" (the previously-open Option B), "is overdue today" (Option A), or a reading of current `PaymentObligation.status`. **Interaction with Open Decision 3, preserved distinctly:** OD3 determines the historical status at the reporting boundary; OD8 determines the derived Overdue condition at that same boundary — the two are not merged into a new alternative.


### 5.1 Historical Obligation Status Semantics (new in v1.1.0, investigation performed this turn)

**Finding, from direct inspection of `app/Domain/Services/ObligationAllocationService.php` and the certified `audit_logs` schema:** `PaymentObligation` carries only a single *current* `status` column — there is no status-history table. However, **every certified status transition is independently audited**: `transitionToTerminalState()` (skip/cancel) and `recalculateStatus()` (triggered by allocate/removeAllocation, producing PENDING → PARTIALLY_PAID → PAID transitions) both write an `OBLIGATION_STATUS_CHANGED` `AuditLog` row with `entity_type = PaymentObligation::class`, `entity_id`, `old_values`/`new_values` (JSON, including the status), and a `created_at` timestamp (confirmed live this turn — `audit_logs` has `entity_type`/`entity_id` indexed together, `old_values`/`new_values` as JSON columns, `created_at` via `useCurrent()`). **Obligation creation itself is not separately audited** (`PaymentObligationService::createRecurringOccurrence()`/`createOneTime()` do not write an `AuditLog` row), but both certified creation paths hardcode the initial status to `'PENDING'`, so the creation-time baseline is always known without needing an explicit log entry for it.

**Conclusion: historical, as-of-a-past-date obligation status reconstruction is technically possible without any schema change**, via: baseline `'PENDING'` at `PaymentObligation.created_at`, then apply the `new_values.status` of the most recent `OBLIGATION_STATUS_CHANGED` audit log entry with `created_at <= [as-of date]`, if any. **This is not yet implemented anywhere, is not a trivial "simple current-status query," and its own correctness (audit-log completeness, JSON-field query performance, timezone alignment between `audit_logs.created_at` and the report's date semantics) would need its own dedicated verification and tests if approved** — this document does not authorize or specify that implementation, it only establishes that the option is real, not merely theoretical.

**This produced a three-way decision, now FROZEN, v1.3.0 (Open Decision 3 — Option B):**
- ~~A. Current-state status reporting~~ — not chosen.
- **B. Historical/as-of-period status reporting — FROZEN.** Reconstructed via the `audit_logs` mechanism above, per the reconstruction rule now stated in full in §4.3: baseline `PENDING`, apply the latest applicable `OBLIGATION_STATUS_CHANGED` transition at or before the as-of boundary, else remain `PENDING`.
- ~~C. Hybrid~~ — not chosen.

This distinction applies to **every** status value named in §4.3 (`Planned`, `Paid`, `Partially Paid`, `Pending`, `Skipped`, `Overdue`, `Cancelled`), not only `Overdue` — `Overdue` additionally has its own, further-nested question, resolved separately as Open Decision 8 (§4.3).

**Audit Completeness Invariant (new, v1.1.2) — a required distinction, not yet satisfied by this turn's investigation.** The v1.1.0 finding above established only that historical reconstruction is **technically reconstructable from the existing architecture** (a certified `audit_logs` table exists, the relevant services write to it for the transitions inspected). It did **not** establish that the mechanism is **proven complete and safe for historical reporting** — those are two different claims, and this document must not conflate them. A reconstruction mechanism that silently under-counts or mis-orders transitions would be worse than Option A's honestly-labeled "current status only," because it would present a *wrong* historical value with the appearance of precision. Before Open Decision 3's Option B or C could be approved for implementation, the following must be independently verified — none of it has been verified as of v1.1.2, this list only defines what "proven complete" would require:
- **PENDING → PARTIALLY_PAID** transitions are captured for every allocation, not only the first.
- **PARTIALLY_PAID → PAID** transitions are captured, including when the final allocation exactly zeroes the remaining planned amount.
- **Allocation-removal transitions** (an allocation is removed, potentially reverting `PAID`/`PARTIALLY_PAID` back toward `PENDING`) are captured symmetrically to allocation-addition transitions — the certified `recalculateStatus()` path must be confirmed (not assumed) to audit-log both directions.
- **PENDING → SKIPPED** transitions (via `transitionToTerminalState()`) are captured.
- **PENDING → CANCELLED** transitions (via `transitionToTerminalState()`) are captured.
- **Repeated, multiple, or same-day transitions** on a single obligation (e.g., two partial allocations recorded minutes apart, or an allocation followed by its removal followed by a new allocation, all within one day) are each individually captured with correctly-ordered timestamps, not merged or overwritten.
- **Timestamp ordering** is reliable enough to answer "what was the status at moment D" unambiguously even when multiple audit rows share the same `created_at` value at whatever timestamp precision the column actually stores (an ambiguity here would silently corrupt every as-of-date reconstruction).
- **Report-period-boundary reconstruction correctness** — the specific query pattern (baseline `PENDING` at `created_at`, then the latest `OBLIGATION_STATUS_CHANGED` row with `created_at <= boundary`) must be proven correct at the exact edges Phase 6 reports will query against (the first and last instant of a reporting period), not only at arbitrary interior points.

**Until this coverage is independently verified with dedicated tests, "historically reconstructable" (established, v1.1.0) must not be treated as equivalent to "safe to use for historical reporting" (still unconfirmed).** **v1.3.0 — Open Decision 3 is now frozen to Option B, but this Audit Completeness Invariant is explicitly PRESERVED, not weakened or removed by that ruling.** Freezing Option B answers *which* status-reporting model is used; it does not itself constitute the required independent verification that the `audit_logs` mechanism is complete. The Testing Strategy (§13) MUST include a dedicated Audit Completeness verification pass covering every item above before the reconstruction may be relied upon for any published report figure — these testing requirements remain mandatory and are not deleted merely because OD3 is now resolved.

### 5.2 Historical Budget Mutability (new in v1.1.1, source-code verification performed this turn)

v1.1.0 §14 asserted, without citing evidence, that "historical `Budget` rows are immutable... no mismatch is possible by construction." **This claim was not verified when written and is corrected here after direct inspection of the certified Phase 4 implementation.** Seven factual questions, each answered by direct source inspection this turn, not inference:

1. **Can an existing `Budget` row be edited after its period has ended?** **Yes.** `BudgetService::updateBudget()` performs its ownership check, its overlap check (excluding the budget's own prior period), and then `$budget->update([...])` — at no point does it read `$budget->period_end` against the current date or reject the update on that basis.
2. **Is there any explicit historical-budget lock?** **No.** Grepped `BudgetService.php` for `AuditLog`/`booted(`/`ImmutableRecordException` — zero matches. Grepped `app/Models/Budget.php` for the same — zero matches. `Budget` has no `booted()` method at all, unlike `Transaction`/`LedgerEntry`, which explicitly throw `ImmutableRecordException` on `updating`/`deleting`.
3. **Can `budget_amount` be changed after the reporting period?** **Yes** — same update path, no restriction; `UpdateBudgetRequest`'s only rule on the amount is `['required', 'numeric', 'min:0']`, with no time-based condition.
4. **Can `period_start`/`period_end` be changed?** **Yes** — `UpdateBudgetRequest` validates only `'period_end' => ['required', 'date', 'after_or_equal:period_start']` (internal ordering, not "in the future" or "not already ended"), and `updateBudget()` writes both fields unconditionally (subject only to the overlap-uniqueness check against the user's *other* budgets, not any date-in-the-past check).
5. **Is any historical budget version/snapshot/audit mechanism available?** **No.** No relation from `Budget` to `AuditLog` exists; no code path anywhere in the certified codebase calls `AuditLog::create()` for a budget change (contrast with `TransactionService::recordAdjustment()` and `ObligationAllocationService`, both of which do audit-log their respective actions). An `update()` call overwrites the row in place with no trace of the prior value.
6. **Do existing tests prove historical budgets are immutable?** **No.** `BudgetCrudTest.php` contains exactly 5 tests (`test_a_user_can_create_a_budget`, `test_a_budget_can_accept_a_zero_amount`, `test_creating_a_budget_rejects_a_negative_amount`, `test_a_user_can_edit_their_own_budget`, `test_there_is_no_delete_route_for_budgets`) — the one edit test (`test_a_user_can_edit_their_own_budget`) actually *proves the opposite*: it changes `budget_amount` from `5000.00` to `7500.00` and asserts the change succeeds, with no period-based restriction anywhere in the test. `BudgetOwnershipTest.php`/`BudgetOverlapTest.php` cover tenant isolation and concurrency, not historical immutability.
7. **If historical values can be changed, does the database retain the original historical value anywhere?** **No** — per findings 2 and 5, there is no snapshot, version table, or audit-log entry capturing what a `Budget` row's `budget_amount`/`period_start`/`period_end` were before an edit.

**Conclusion: historical `Budget` rows are mutable by the certified Phase 4 implementation, with no retained history of prior values.** The v1.1.0 immutability claim is false and is withdrawn. This is not itself an implementation defect to fix in this turn (per explicit instruction, Phase 4 code is not modified) — it is a factual finding that creates a genuine new reporting-semantics question, recorded as Open Decision 11.

**Whether the frozen Knowledge Base already resolves this question:** checked `01_BUSINESS_RULES.md` BR-055 ("Historical Protection: Changing a template, category rule, or current configuration must not silently rewrite committed historical transactions") and BR-057 ("Correction Preference: Prefer reversal, refund, adjustment, or controlled correction mechanisms over destructive edits when the change represents a financial event"). **Neither rule names `Budget` specifically, and neither was applied to budgets during Phase 4's certification** (confirmed: Phase 4's own Decision Package/Implementation Report never invoked BR-055/BR-057 for budget edits, only for the already-certified transaction-immutability and obligation-historical-protection guarantees). Applying BR-055/BR-057 to budgets now, retroactively, would be reinterpreting a frozen rule's scope after the fact — exactly what this document must not do silently. **No existing frozen document resolves this question for `Budget` specifically; a new Open Decision is required**, not a citation of an already-settled answer.

**§5.2b — Expanded scenario analysis (new, v1.1.2).** v1.1.1 established mutability as a fact but analyzed it only as a single, undifferentiated "the amount might have changed" risk. Independent review for v1.1.2 identified that mutability actually produces **two distinct dimensions of drift**, not one — a `Budget` row's *amount* can change independently of whether its *identity as belonging to the requested reporting period* changes. These must be separated, because a reporting engine could resolve one without resolving the other, and v1.1.1's "Option A" (accept current stored values) is itself underspecified once period mutation is considered: "current stored values" presumes the edited row still *is* the row for the requested period, which is not guaranteed. Five sub-scenarios, all technically possible via the same unrestricted `BudgetService::updateBudget()` path documented in §5.2's findings 1–4 above:

1. **Amount-only mutation.** `budget_amount` is edited after the period has ended; `period_start`/`period_end` are unchanged. The edited budget still unambiguously belongs to the requested reporting period — only its value is in question.
2. **`period_start` mutation.** The budget's start date is moved (forward or backward) after the fact, potentially causing it to no longer cover the beginning of a previously-reported range, or to newly cover a range it didn't before.
3. **`period_end` mutation.** Symmetric to (2) — the budget's end date is moved, potentially truncating or extending its coverage of a previously-reported range.
4. **Combined amount + period mutation.** Both the value and the period identity change in the same edit — the two forms of drift compound, and a report cannot treat them as independently resolvable (e.g., "use the current amount, but the original period" is not a coherent instruction once both have changed).
5. **Budget moved entirely outside the requested reporting range.** The most severe case: `period_start`/`period_end` are edited such that the budget row no longer overlaps the range a historical report is being run against *at all*, even though it did at the time the period elapsed. A report for that original range must decide whether the budget "still exists" for reporting purposes.

**Worked example (illustrating scenarios 1, 3, and 5 together):** a user creates a Budget of ₹5,000 for August 1–31. The period elapses; a Phase 6 report is later run for August. Independently of that report:
- If the user then edits the budget's `budget_amount` to ₹6,000 (scenario 1) — does the August report show ₹5,000 (the value in effect during August) or ₹6,000 (the current stored value)? Neither figure is retained anywhere once the edit is made — only ₹6,000 exists in the database.
- If the user instead (or additionally) edits `period_end` to September 30, extending the budget into September (scenario 3/4) — does an August report still see a "₹5,000-for-August" budget, or does the budget now read as a single ₹5,000/₹6,000 budget spanning August–September, making an August-only figure impossible to isolate without also deciding how to prorate or attribute it?
- If the user instead moves the entire budget to September 1–30 (scenario 5) — an August report run *after* this edit finds **no Budget row at all** covering August, even though one existed and was presumably used for utilization tracking throughout August. The August report would show either an empty/missing Budget line, or nothing, depending on how "period membership" (Open Decision 7) is itself resolved — these two open questions compound.

**The reporting question, now FROZEN, v1.3.0 — both sub-decisions resolved to Option A:**

- **11.A — Historical amount semantics — FROZEN (Option A: accept current stored value).** For historical reporting, the report uses the **currently stored** `budget_amount`. There is no historical budget-version reconstruction, no attempt to infer the value that existed when the reporting period originally occurred, and no version-history mechanism authorized by this decision. Worked example: a Budget originally ₹5,000 for August, later edited to ₹6,000 — a historical August report shows **₹6,000**, because that is the currently stored value. **This is an explicit acceptance of the certified database reality, not a claim of historical immutability** — the original ₹5,000 value is not recoverable, and this ruling does not describe it as recoverable, does not introduce an audit/version table, and does not modify Phase 4 `Budget` behavior.
- **11.B — Historical period/identity semantics — FROZEN (Option A: accept current stored period).** Historical reporting uses the `Budget` row's **currently stored** `period_start`/`period_end`. The report does not reconstruct period boundaries that existed in the past. Worked example: a Budget originally August 1–31, later changed to September 1–30 — an August report executed after that edit uses the current stored period (September), and per Open Decision 7's Overlap semantics (§4.2/§4.3), **the budget no longer overlaps August and is not included** in an August report. **Combined mutation:** if `budget_amount`, `period_start`, and `period_end` are all changed together, the report uses the current stored values for all of them — no partial reconstruction of "the amount as it was during the original period" is attempted.

**Neither sub-decision introduces historical reconstruction, versioning, or any change to Phase 4 `Budget` code.** Options B (freeze historical rows) and C (versioning/snapshots) — previously open alternatives for both 11.A and 11.B — are no longer under consideration; the certified factual finding above (Budget rows are mutable, no retained prior value) stands, and this ruling is an explicit acceptance of that reality, not a resolution that pretends otherwise.

## 8. Performance Architecture

- **SQL vs. PHP aggregation:** proposed to mirror `BudgetService`'s certified pattern — `SELECT SUM(...)` aggregate queries via `DB::table()`/query builder, not fetch-then-sum-in-PHP, for every count/sum figure. **Opening/Closing Balance — FROZEN, v1.3.0 (Open Decision 2, Option B):** implemented as a new date-bounded calculation inside `ReportingService`, analogous in shape to `SafeToSpendService::calculateCurrentAssetBalance()`'s certified per-account-loop pattern (looping the combined selected accounts, per frozen Open Decision 14-C), not a raw single aggregate query — this is the resolved architecture, not a pending exception.
- **Eager loading:** any per-obligation/per-budget/per-account report row must eager-load its `category`/`account`/`plannedAccount` relationships, per the identical certified pattern in `PaymentObligationController::index()`/`BudgetController::index()`.
- **Indexes:** `transactions` already carries `(user_id, transaction_date)` and `(user_id, transaction_type)` composite indexes (confirmed live this turn) — directly usable for every proposed date-range/type-filtered query without a new migration. `ledger_entries` carries `(user_id, transaction_id)` and `(user_id, account_id)` — a date-bounded balance query joins through `transactions` first (using its date index), then to `ledger_entries` via the existing `(user_id, transaction_id)` index. **No new index is proposed as definitely required**, but this is not empirically verified via `EXPLAIN` against realistic data volume — flagged as a verification step for the implementation turn, not a blocker for this Decision Package. If `EXPLAIN` later reveals a full scan, a new index proposal would require its own separate approval per section 15's explicit process — not pre-authorized here. **No migration is required merely to support the historical balance calculation** (Open Decision 2, Option B) — it is computed entirely from existing ledger data.
- **Date-range limits / chunking — FROZEN, v1.2.0/v1.3.0 (Open Decision 4, fully resolved):** maximum report date range = **one year**; this bounds both query cost and memory for the historical balance calculation and every other date-scoped query.
- **Pagination:** Cash Flow/Budget/Obligations report *rows* (e.g., a list of obligations in the Obligations report) should paginate identically to the certified `PaymentObligationController::index()` pattern (`->paginate(20)`); *aggregate* figures (Opening Balance, single Income/Expense totals) are single values, not paginated, consistent with how Phase 5's dashboard cards work.
- **Caching:** not proposed, for the identical reason Phase 5 rejected it — no frozen document authorizes it, and every certified calculation in this codebase to date is deliberately recomputed per request. If historical reports prove expensive enough to warrant caching, that is a separate, explicitly-approved architectural decision, not assumed here.

    **E. Double-Entry Treatment — CRITICAL. Verified architectural finding, unaffected by this ruling.** Verified by direct inspection of every ledger-writing service, not assumed:
    - `TransactionService::recordExpense()` / `recordIncome()` / `recordAdjustment()` each create **exactly one** `LedgerEntry` — EXPENSE, INCOME, and ADJUSTMENT transactions touch **one** Account only. There is no "other leg" for these types; no double-counting risk exists for them under any account scope.
    - `RefundService::refund()` creates **exactly one** `LedgerEntry` (INFLOW, on the refund's own destination account) — same single-leg structure as above.
    - `TransferService::transfer()` creates **exactly two** `LedgerEntry` rows — one OUTFLOW on the source account, one INFLOW on the destination account, always two distinct accounts, both asserted to be owned by the acting user. **This is the only transaction type in the certified schema that is genuinely two-account.**
    - `ReversalService::reverse()` mirrors **every** ledger entry of its parent transaction, direction-inverted — so a Reversal-of-an-Expense/Income/Adjustment/Refund is single-leg (inheriting its parent's one leg), while a **Reversal-of-a-Transfer is two-leg** (inheriting the Transfer's two legs) — confirmed by direct inspection of `ReversalService::reverse()`'s `foreach ($parentEntries as $entry)` loop, which iterates however many entries the parent actually has.
    - **Conclusion: the double-counting risk this sub-part must analyze is narrower than "every transaction type" — it applies only to `TRANSFER` transactions and `REVERSAL`-of-`TRANSFER` transactions**, because those are the only transaction types whose two ledger legs can both fall inside a selected multi-account (or "all accounts") scope simultaneously.
